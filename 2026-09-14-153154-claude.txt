  Key finding: the standard Kratky diagnostics are confounded here, but for an 
  interesting reason.

  1. Guinier fits give Rg ≈ 500–530 Å for both protein and blank, nearly 
     identical between them. That's two orders of magnitude larger than a folded
     protein domain (tens of Å) — this is picking up large-scale
     aggregate/inhomogeneity (or channel/capillary) scattering at the very
     lowest q, shared equally by protein and buffer. Since it's the same in both
     curves, it's not protein-specific signal, and it makes the dimensionless
     (Receveur-Carrey) Kratky plot uninformative here: with Rg this large, the
     diagnostic qRg=0–5 window only spans raw q≈0–0.01 Å⁻¹, nowhere near the
     WAXS peak region we actually care about — both curves show the same tiny,
     indistinguishable bump right at that huge shared low-q feature (visible in
     the right panels of figures/*__kratky.png).
  2. The raw Kratky plot (I·q²) is dominated by the un-decayed edge at q≈2 Å⁻¹ —
     a known Kratky-plot artifact when the measured q-range doesn't fully decay
     to a flat baseline by qmax (ours extends into WAXS, well beyond the usual
     SAXS-only Kratky range). My crude "high-q plateau / peak ratio" metric
     (43–70, all ≫1) is really just reporting that un-decayed edge, not genuine
     disorder — not a reliable classifier here.
  3. What the q²-weighting does usefully do: it suppresses the huge low-q region
     and amplifies the mid/high-q region, giving a cleaner complementary view
     of the same WAXS-region difference we found before. The green
     (protein−blank) curve in
     figures/y2f_protein_vs_kpi_buffer_blank__kratky.png shows a positive excess
     and figures/yr2a_protein_vs_kpi_buffer_blank__kratky.png shows a negative 
     dip in the same q≈1.1–1.7 Å⁻¹ region — directionally consistent with what
     the difference-curve analysis found earlier (Y2F excess, YR2A depression),
     from a completely independent representation. That consistency across two
     different analysis approaches is somewhat reassuring, though given the
     peak-significance numbers didn't survive the baseline-artifact fix, I'd
     still treat the magnitude as unconfirmed — only the direction/sign of the
     effect is corroborated twice now.

  Bottom line: Kratky analysis isn't well-suited to this dataset in its standard
  form (the huge shared low-q Rg breaks the dimensionless version, and the
  extended WAXS range breaks the raw version's decay assumption), but it does
  independently confirm the sign/location of the earlier tentative Y2F vs YR2A

  each of the 9 flow-channel conditions by rotator "position N" and fits Rg,

⏺ Deep dive: Y2F_kpi_h2o vs kpi_h2o — found a bug, then a serious confound

  1. Fixed a second copy of the baseline-anchoring bug

  The earlier baseline-anchoring fix (DIFF_Q_BASE) was applied to the main
  peak-significance test but I'd missed applying it to the replicate-level peak
  calculations in protein_vs_buffer.py (lines computing
  rep_heights/rep_heights_sc) — they were still using peak_analysis's default
  baseline windows, which are wrong for difference curves. This is why the
  printed "replicate variance" range looked wild ([-0.00824, 0.467], huge
  spread, sign flips). Fixed by passing q_base=DIFF_Q_BASE there too.

  After the fix, all 181 single-replicate diffs (under i0 normalization) give a
  positive peak height, tightly clustered (0.006–0.102, mean 0.043, no sign
  flips). Frame-level consistency is good — this part of the result is solid.

  2. But: a time-drift control test undermines confidence in the result

  kpi_h2o (the blank) was measured 18:29–19:13, while Y2F_kpi_h2o was measured
  20:03–20:35 — a 50min–2hr gap, with two unrelated YR2A runs in between
  (syringe swaps). That's a real opportunity for instrumental drift, not protein
  signal, to produce a fake "difference."

  I tested this directly: split the blank condition alone (kpi_h2o, no protein
  at all) into early half (18:29–~18:51) vs late half (~18:51–19:13) and ran the
  identical scale-corrected peak analysis between them.
